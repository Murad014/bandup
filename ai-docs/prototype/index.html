The Return of the Wolves

✏️ Select text to highlight it. Tap a highlight to remove it. Tap a dotted word for the Azerbaijani meaning.

By the 1920s, grey wolves had been completely eliminated from Yellowstone National Park in the United States, largely through government-sponsored hunting. Without their main predator, elk populations expanded rapidly and overgrazed young willow and aspen trees along riverbanks. In 1995, fourteen wolves captured in Canada were released into the park, and a further seventeen followed the next year. Many scientists claim the outcome was a classic example of a "trophic cascade", in which changes at the top of a food chain ripple down to affect the entire ecosystem. As elk numbers fell and the animals avoided open valleys, vegetation recovered, which in turn provided habitat for beavers and songbirds. Nevertheless, this narrative has been challenged. Some ecologists point out that harsh winters, growing bear populations and human hunting also reduced elk numbers during the same period, making it difficult to attribute the changes to wolves alone.
